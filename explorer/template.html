How crop models project climate change to shift yields in Africa

This page summarises a curated database of published crop-model projections for African sites. Each record is one model run: a crop, a location, a climate scenario and time slice, with or without an adaptation practice, reporting the projected yield change (%) against the study's own simulated baseline. Local warming (ΔT) at each site is harmonised from the NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 ensemble so that runs from different studies sit on one temperature axis.

Dataset curated by Namita Joshi for a systematic review of crop-model projections in Africa. The analysis is unpublished; please contact the author before citing figures.

Points are model runs. Lines are quadratic fits per practice. Bands are 95% intervals from 1,000 bootstrap draws resampled within studies. A line is drawn only when a practice has at least 10 runs, 2 studies and a 1 °C spread of ΔT. Hover a line or point for values.

3 · Read before using

Methods and caveats

  • Relative, not absolute. Yield change is the percentage difference between each study's simulated future and simulated baseline yield. Baselines are model yields under each study's assumed management, which may sit closer to attainable than to farm-level actual yield. Applying these percentages to observed yields assumes the proportional response carries over.
  • Warming axis. ΔT is local mean-temperature change at the study coordinates from the NEX-GDDP-CMIP6 35-model ensemble (temp_change_nextgddp), not the scenario label the study reported. Runs without coordinates or a matched delta are excluded from the curves.
  • Horizons. Near = future mid-point up to 2040, Mid = 2041–2070, End = after 2070.
  • Practices. Variety = any cultivar change. Fertilizer = all fertiliser levels. Planting date = early, late or shifted sowing. Bundled = two or more practices combined. Mulch and "other" are mapped in the coverage table but not fitted.
  • Outliers. Runs beyond 3× the interquartile range within each crop × practice are dropped before fitting, as in the source analysis.
  • Uncertainty is a floor. The bootstrap resamples runs within each study, so the bands describe sampling of runs, not disagreement between studies or models. With few studies the curve shape can be driven by one paper; check the study count before quoting a value.
  • Pooled sources. Records come from a Web of Science / Scopus search plus earlier compilations (Carr et al., Aggarwal et al., Hasegawa et al.). Studies differ in whether their crop models include CO₂ fertilisation, and the curves pool both.
